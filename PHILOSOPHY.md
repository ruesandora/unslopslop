# How unslopslop thinks

## Two ways to fail

Picture three plates of chicken.

1. **Half-cooked.** The typical AI page: purple gradient, glowing button, three identical cards, "Unleash seamless workflows". Something was cooked, but nobody decided anything. This is **slop**.
2. **Raw.** The over-corrected page: every effect removed, a beige background, a default serif, one narrow column of text, nothing moves, no image. No slop is left, and nothing else either. This is **bland**, and it is just as much a failure.
3. **Marinated.** The same chicken, prepared for one particular dish: a sauce made for it, layers of flavour, a plate that suits it. This is what a good studio ships: a direction chosen for this product, with depth, tone, texture, a real visual and motion that explains something.

The detector therefore reports two numbers. **Slop** (lower is better) counts the default moves. **Craft** (higher is better) measures hierarchy, a focal point, a real visual, purposeful motion, identity and density. A page passes only with low slop **and** high craft. Zero slop on a bland page is a fail.

## A sauce for each dish

There is no house style. A meditation timer, a fintech dashboard, a children's reading app and a record label's site each need a different direction, palette, texture, motion character and density. The first step of every job is to read the project (`skills/unslopslop/references/project-read.md`): who uses it, in what mood, on what device, what the product actually does. Only then is a direction chosen.

The same product type can go many ways. An invoicing tool, for example:

| Mood | Direction | Combination | Texture | Motion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calm, exact (freelancers) | Night ledger: ink stage, paper documents, editorial serif | ink + bone + cobalt (action only) | fine grain, ledger lines in one band | one demo of the reminder timeline, then still |
| Warm, friendly (small shops) | Paper and stamps: cream ground, rubber-stamp marks | cream + tomato + bottle green | paper fibre, stamp edges | stamps land once with a small overshoot |
| Fast, technical (agencies) | Console: dense, mono for ids only | graphite + white + signal yellow | none, sharp 1 px lines | instant state swaps, 150 ms |

None of these is "correct". Each is correct for a project.

## Colour in combinations

In the early 1930s, the Japanese painter and costume designer Sanzo Wada (1883-1967) set out to record the colour sensibility of his time in a six-volume work, *Haishoku Soukan*. Rather than listing single colours, he paired them in sets of two, three and four, so that every colour was always seen in company. A selection was later reissued as *A Dictionary of Color Combinations*, which designers still treat as a quiet cult reference. Its lesson is simple: no colour is right or wrong alone, and meaning comes from the combination and its context.

Our reading of it: a hue that looks awkward by itself can be exactly right beside the right neighbour, and the most "classy" colour can look absurd in the wrong place. Even a purple nobody likes can be perfect in the right combination. unslopslop borrows this mindset, not any book's palettes. Like a cook who marinates the same chicken differently for each dish, the skill picks colours for the project in front of it instead of reaching for the same default gradient. We are inspired by Sanzo Wada and his work and are not affiliated with his estate or publishers.

In practice: think in sets of two to four colours with roles and proportions, never in single swatches. Method: `skills/unslopslop/references/palette-combinations.md`.

## What craft is made of

- **Hierarchy:** one display size that commits, clear steps below it.
- **A focal point:** the eye knows where to land first.
- **A real visual:** the product itself, a photo, a drawing with a subject. Never a blurred orb.
- **Layers and depth:** surfaces that overlap, soft layered shadows, a second and third tone.
- **Texture:** grain, a subtle pattern or watermark in one zone, a directional light behind the subject. Quiet, under 10% opacity.
- **Purposeful motion:** one written moment that explains how the product behaves (a demo as a pure function of time, `render(t)`), then stillness. Respect reduced motion.
- **Identity:** a chosen type pairing, a colour combination with roles, one signature move nobody else would make.
- **Density that fits:** app screens dense and calm, landing pages balanced, reading pages airy.

## Honesty

No invented numbers, logos, testimonials or users. Example data is labelled as example data. If content is missing, a placeholder is marked and reported, not dressed up.
