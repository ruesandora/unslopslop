Prompt-led product reel

A short product film where typed commands carry the story. A dark terminal resolves a checklist line by line, a light title darkens word by word, then a product window slides in with a slight turn while a command chip on its bottom edge types the request and the result appears.

Measured beats
Command typed at about 40 characters per second. Checklist: a new line every 400 ms, arriving dim and reaching full tone in 250 ms; the last line in the success colour. Title: each word goes from a pale tone to ink in 220 ms, 120 ms apart. Window: slides 60 px and turns from 10 degrees to 0 in 700 ms ease-out, the label pill above it fades in 200 ms earlier. Chip text at 40 characters per second with a block cursor that blinks every 530 ms; the result appears 300 ms after the last character.
How it is built
render(t) sets every scene from the time alone: which scene is visible, how many characters are typed, each line's tone, each word's tone, the window transform. A scrubber and a Play button drive it. Export frames by stepping t; a test can assert any frame.
Where it fits
Launch films for tools and agents, a demo loop in a hero, a 4:5 social cut. The commands should be real requests the product handles.
Reduced motion
Shows the final frame of the last scene.
Becomes slop when
The commands are vague ("make it better"), the window shows a fake interface with nothing in it, or the typing cursor keeps blinking after the film ends.
